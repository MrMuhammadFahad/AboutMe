  • 01 / Master 1 research project · 2025–2026

    NeuroSynth

    Research platformA full-stack system for generating, validating and evaluating synthetic 8-channel EEG across six cognitive and clinical classes. The study compares an LLM-and-Fourier pipeline with a baseline diffusion model and a frequency-guided diffusion approach.

    Three synthesis methodsLLM + Fourier: Claude returns structured EEG frequency parameters; a Fourier engine synthesizes the waveform, then physiological quality checks validate it and retry failures. DDPM baseline: a class-conditional 1D U-Net learns to generate signals. MSC + DFSI: an enhanced diffusion model combines multi-scale temporal convolutions with diffusion-step-aware frequency guidance.

    Data & evaluationReal EEG comes from PhysioNet, CHB-MIT, Sleep-EDF and cognitive-task data. Each example contains eight channels over 10 seconds at 256 Hz. Simple CNN and EEGNet classifiers test real-to-synthetic, synthetic-to-real and mixed-data transfer, alongside a real-to-real baseline. Signal similarity is assessed with power spectral density, Pearson correlation and Hjorth parameters; classification is reported with per-class precision, recall and F1.

    Research contributionTests whether generated EEG is not only plausible in signal statistics, but also useful for downstream classification and data augmentation. Preprocessing and evaluation use reproducible splits, with normalization fitted on training data to avoid leakage.

    1. 01Prepare real EEG
    2. 02Generate with 3 methods
    3. 03Compare signals & classifiers

    Developed for the M1 Semester 2 project at Université Paris-Est Créteil. The repository is private; access is available on request.

  • 02 / Computer vision

    Pose-based gait analysis

    PurposeTransforms ordinary walking footage into a compact, structured gait profile using markerless pose estimation and temporal movement analysis.

    Video-to-features pipelinePython and MediaPipe Pose detect body landmarks frame by frame. The analysis follows hip, knee and ankle keypoints to calculate joint angles and derive step duration, cadence and ankle movement from the walking sequence.

    Why it mattersInstead of relying on a visual impression alone, the project represents gait as eight measurable features that can be inspected, compared across recordings, or used as input to subsequent analysis. It demonstrates a practical path from computer-vision landmarks to interpretable movement metrics.
